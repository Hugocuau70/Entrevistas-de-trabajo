Role play · discovery

Discovery questions to map the paper process

Decision
  • Who signs, and what is the approval threshold?
  • Who else needs to agree? Who could stop this?
  • What decision criteria matter most: cost, control, safety?
Process
  • Vendor onboarding steps and documents (RFC, bank details, compliance).
  • Independence check: is it required for a non-client vendor?
  • Legal: our terms or theirs? Data-processing addendum?
Budget and timing
  • Which cost center pays for travel today, and how much?
  • Is the 2027 budget closed? Can this fit within current travel spend?
  • Target go-live and what happens if it slips to February.
Current state
  • Taxi, app or fleet providers in use; any contract to exit?
  • Expense tool and approval flow; average claims per month.

Role play · objections

Expected pushback and how to answer

ObjectionResponse: fact, then question back
"Clients won't accept travel on the invoice."Rebilling is optional per engagement letter; the codes still give cost visibility by client. Which clients already pay travel today?
"We need CFDI for deductibility."We agree the invoicing setup (CFDI) with your tax team before go-live, in writing. Who should join that call?
"The platform reform makes Uber unstable or pricier."Drivers are registered with IMSS; service continues. Fares rose up to 7% in July 2026 and the forecast includes it. What price point would worry you?
"Staff will use it for personal trips."Separate profiles, ride policies by time and place, flags and manager approval. What rule would you set first?
"Not now, we're entering busy season."That is the reason to start now: a pilot in two offices by January 4 needs about two hours of your team's time. Who can own it?
Confirm CFDI and data-privacy answers with Uber Tax and Legal

Revenue analysis

How the GB forecast is built

Monthly GB = rides × USD 10 + Eats orders × USD 15
  • Rides = client-facing staff × rides per person (busy or off season) + other staff × 0.5
  • Eats = client-facing staff × vouchers × participation (Jan–May); 0.1 orders per employee otherwise
  • Projected = potential × adoption curve after go-live (20% → 70% by month 6)

Defaults: 1,000 employees, 65% client-facing, 4 rides/month in busy season and 2 off season, 2 Eats vouchers/month at 35% participation, go-live in January.

Win plan

What must be true to close in December

Must be true
  • CFO sees rebilling and control as margin, not cost.
  • HR sponsors the late-night policy as duty of care.
  • IT can deliver the roster and engagement-code files in one week.
  • The independence and vendor check fits in two weeks.
Risks and mitigation
  • Already on DiDi Empresas or a taxi contract: pilot side by side in two offices.
  • Coverage in industrial parks: Central for scheduled trips; check before the meeting.
  • Decision slips to January: pilot Mexico City only, scale in February.
  • Already a U4B customer: reframe as expansion (Eats, Central, SFTP).
